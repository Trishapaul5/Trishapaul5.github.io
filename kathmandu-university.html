I worked as a virtual research intern on a cross-cultural study of graphic design app reviews, called “Cultural Dissonance Analysis in Graphic Design App Reviews”. It used Hofstede’s cultural dimensions to compare user feedback in South Asian, Southeast Asian and Western markets.

View proof document ↗

What I did and what it achieved

Work

  • Scraped one- and two-star reviews of graphic design apps with Python, google-play-scraper, BeautifulSoup and Selenium across 19 Asian and 6 Western countries.
  • Cleaned the text with a 10-step NLP pipeline, including tokenisation, lemmatisation and stop-word removal.
  • Used UTF-16 encoding so Hindi, Bengali, Thai, Indonesian, Portuguese and Arabic reviews stayed readable.
  • Built LDA topic models for South Asia, Southeast Asia and the combined data, tuned with four coherence metrics.
  • Checked each topic by mapping real, unprocessed reviews from every country back to it.
  • Extracted language features for Hofstede’s six dimensions, using sentiment and emotion analysis, and compared countries with PCA plots and heatmaps.

Impact

  • Collected and prepared 290,000+ multilingual reviews from 25 countries.
  • Surfaced 21 themes, with coherence scores of 0.45–0.48.
  • Cut data loss by about 40% in multilingual data by fixing the encoding.
  • Showed that Western reviews scored about 3.7 times higher on individualism than Asian reviews.
  • Flagged login and account problems as a high-priority issue in Southeast Asia and suggested region-specific localisation.
  • Delivered three reports: LDA analysis, coherence optimisation and Hofstede insights.
